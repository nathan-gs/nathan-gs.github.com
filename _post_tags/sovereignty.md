Digital sovereignty is less about where something was manufactured than about who gets to decide how it is used. Supply chain *dependency* — the silicon, the accelerators, the firmware — is for most organisations unavoidable. *Dependence* — a control plane, keys, or an audit trail held by someone else — is a choice, and usually a reversible one.

The European Commission's Cloud Sovereignty Framework scores this across eight objectives (strategic, legal, data & AI, operational, supply chain, technology, security, sustainability) on SEAL levels 0–4; Scrydon's [Cloud Sovereignty Score](https://scrydon.com/sovereignty/score/cloud) implements it as a self-assessment. Notably, supply chain carries the smallest weight of the major objectives — operational and technology sovereignty carry more.

The same argument scales all the way down to a home battery: see [#Home Assistant]({% include tag_url tag="Home Assistant" %}) and [#Home Energy Management]({% include tag_url tag="Home Energy Management" %}), and up to a [#AI OS]({% include tag_url tag="AI OS" %}) running air-gapped or on-premise.
